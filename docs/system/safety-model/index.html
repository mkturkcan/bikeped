    Safety model¶

    The testbench needs a ground truth for "this frame is dangerous" that does not depend on the decision pipeline. It gets one from a kinematic model built on cyclist braking profiles, a pedestrian reaction time and a conflict zone around the pedestrian. This page lists the constants with the sources cited in the code and states the labelling rule exactly.

    Cyclist braking profiles¶

    A profile is a perception-reaction time and a braking deceleration (CyclistBrakingProfile). The stopping distance is

    d_stop = v * prt_s + v^2 / (2 * decel_ms2)
    Key PRT (s) Deceleration (m/s²) Source given in the code
    bicycle 0.84 1.96 Martin & Bigazzi 2025, 85th percentile, field measurements, n = 302 (doi)
    ebike 0.84 6.0 PRT as above; deceleration from Naude et al. 2025, mean maximum in track tests, n = 127 (doi)
    aashto 2.5 3.4 AASHTO, A Policy on Geometric Design of Highways and Streets, 7th ed. (2018)

    bicycle is the default. A scenario selects another profile with its vehicle key, and the profile then drives both the ground-truth labels and the speed-adaptive bound of the pipeline under test, because make_pipeline passes the profile's values to DecisionPipeline. The E-Scooter and Accelerating E-Bike conformance scenarios use ebike. The aashto profile is there for reference against design guidance.

    Stopping distances returned by stopping_distance for each profile:

    Profile 4 m/s 6 m/s 8 m/s 10 m/s
    bicycle 7.44 m 14.22 m 23.05 m 33.91 m
    ebike 4.69 m 8.04 m 12.05 m 16.73 m
    aashto 12.35 m 20.29 m 29.41 m 39.71 m

    Pedestrian and conflict-zone constants¶

    Constant Value Unit Meaning
    PEDESTRIAN_REACTION_S 1.87 s Reaction time of a distracted pedestrian (Fugger et al. 2000, combined average).
    PEDESTRIAN_CLEAR_SPEED_MS 1.3 m/s Walking speed to clear the conflict zone.
    CONFLICT_ZONE_HALF_WIDTH_M 1.0 m Half-width of the zone the pedestrian occupies.
    SWERVE_FRICTION_MU 0.4 Tyre-road friction assumed for a swerve.
    MAX_SEVERITY_SPEED_MS 12.0 m/s Cyclist speed at which severity reaches 1.
    ACTIONABLE_TTC_LOWER 1.87 s Shortest time to collision at which a warning can still help.

    Swerve time¶

    A cyclist can also avoid the pedestrian by moving sideways out of the conflict zone. The code models that as reaction time plus the time to cover the half-width w under constant lateral acceleration a_lat = mu * g:

    a_lat      = 0.4 * 9.81           = 3.92 m/s^2
t_maneuver = sqrt(2 * w / a_lat)  = 0.71 s
t_swerve   = 0.84 + 0.71          = 1.55 s

    The result does not depend on forward speed. It is kept as CYCLIST_SWERVE_TIME_S for comparison, and the actionable bound uses the pedestrian's 1.87 s instead: the system exists to warn the pedestrian, so the labels are judged against the pedestrian's response window.

    Labelling a frame¶

    compute_ground_truth_danger(scenario, t) evaluates every bike and pedestrian pair that is within 25 m of each other at time t and returns one DangerAssessment.

    For each pair it computes:

    • the bike speed v from the scripted path and the stopping distance sd under the scenario's braking profile;
    • from time_to_collision, which scans the next 5 s of both paths in 0.05 s steps: the minimum future distance d_min, the time ttc until the distance first drops below the 1.0 m conflict radius, and the current closing speed v_close;
    • the gap to the conflict zone, max(d_cur - 1.0, 0);
    • a time-to-collision threshold, 1.87 s plus the time the pedestrian needs to walk 2.0 m at their current speed. A pedestrian slower than 0.1 m/s cannot clear the zone and gets 30 s.

    The pair is dangerous when the paths converge and the cyclist either cannot stop in time or is about to arrive:

    is_danger = (
    v_close > 0.3                      # closing, m/s
    and d_min < convergence_m          # 5.0 m: the paths actually converge
    and (sd > gap_to_conflict * stop_margin    # stop_margin = 0.8
         or ttc < ttc_threshold)
)
actionable = is_danger and ttc >= ACTIONABLE_TTC_LOWER
severity   = min((v / MAX_SEVERITY_SPEED_MS) ** 2, 1.0)

    The convergence test keeps parallel paths out: two agents can close on each other for a while and still pass more than 5 m apart. Severity scales with the square of cyclist speed, as kinetic energy does.

    When several pairs are dangerous the function returns the one with the highest severity. When none is, it returns the pair with the smallest d_min.

    The DangerAssessment record¶

    Field Unit Meaning
    is_danger The frame is physically threatening.
    actionable Dangerous, and the time to collision is at least 1.87 s, so a warning can still change the outcome.
    severity 0 to 1 Squared cyclist speed relative to 12 m/s.
    ttc s Time until the pair is within 1.0 m; infinite if that does not happen within 5 s.
    d_min_future m Minimum distance within the 5 s lookahead.
    closing_speed m/s Positive when the distance is shrinking.
    bike_speed m/s Cyclist ground speed.
    stopping_dist m Stopping distance at that speed.
    warning_budget s Time from this frame to closest approach.
    distance m Current distance between the pair.

    How the metrics use the labels¶

    Dangerous frames that are no longer actionable are called imminent in the code. The two tiers are scored differently:

    Metric Definition
    True positive Alert on an actionable frame.
    False negative No alert on an actionable frame.
    False positive Alert on a frame with no danger of either tier.
    True negative No alert on a frame with no danger.
    Sensitivity True positives over actionable frames.
    Specificity True negatives over frames with no danger.
    Severity-weighted FN Summed severity of missed actionable frames over summed severity of all actionable frames.
    Reaction adequacy Share of alert onsets that leave more than 1.87 s before closest approach.

    An alert during an imminent frame counts neither for nor against the system. The alert is correct, and the pedestrian could no longer have used it.
